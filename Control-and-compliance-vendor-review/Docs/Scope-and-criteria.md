# Scope and Assessment Criteria

---

## Objective
Assess whether 15 selected security, privacy, and vendor controls are suitably designed, evidenced, and operating for the fictional Velora Commerce GmbH environment.

---

## Assessment Period
Simulated review of the previous 90 days.

---

## In Scope
- Governance and policy
- Asset and risk management
- MFA and access review
- Vulnerability and configuration management
- Cloud and security-log review
- Incident response
- Backup restoration
- Data classification and retention
- Vendor due diligence and monitoring
- Secret management
- Security awareness
- GDPR-related processing and DPIA screening

---

## Out of Scope
- Formal GDPR legal opinion
- Certification or attestation
- ISO 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS, or financial audit
- Real vendor assurance review
- Production-system access or testing
- Real employee or customer data
- Physical security testing

---

## Assessment Criteria
- Selected outcomes from NIST CSF 2.0
- Selected CIS Controls v8.1 control areas
- GDPR Articles 5, 24, 28, 32, and 35 for limited privacy context
- Velora's fictional risk appetite and Week 17 risk-treatment commitments

---

## Conclusion Boundary
The review reports the state of selected controls based on simulated evidence. It does not certify compliance or provide legal advice.

---

## Assumptions
1. **Fictional Context Boundary:** Velora Commerce GmbH is a simulated, point-in-time e-commerce scenario utilizing an administrative workforce of approximately 60 users; all operational profiles, technical gaps, and regulatory conditions represent modeled variables rather than live audit constraints.
2. **Technical Architecture Baseline:** The core engineering stack relies exclusively on Microsoft 365, Entra ID, Windows 11 enterprise endpoints, GitHub source control repositories, an Ubuntu-based application layer, and managed AWS infrastructure hosted out of the eu-central-1 region.
3. **No Independent Validation Testing:** All control effectiveness evaluations are derived from simulated findings within the provided portfolio dossier rather than active penetration testing, source code inspection, or real-time infrastructure log analysis.
4. **Good Faith Employee Modeling:** The assessment assumes that the existing corporate IT Manager and Engineering Lead execute operations in good faith according to their stated informal procedures, despite documented gaps in formal policy and measurement.
5. **No Parallel Framework Audit:** The scope is intentionally constrained to selected control outcomes within NIST CSF 2.0, CIS Controls v8.1, and limited GDPR privacy principles; it does not constitute a full-scope audit for formal SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, or PCI DSS certification.
6. **Static Environment Variable:** The organizational architecture, asset boundaries, vendor footprints, and data classifications remain static throughout the 90-day simulated assessment window to provide an unfluctuating baseline for qualitative analysis.
7. **Legal Advice Exclusion:** All references to regulatory data handling, retention assumptions, and privacy classifications under GDPR Articles 5, 24, 28, 32, and 35 are for risk management structuring purposes only and do not constitute a formal legal opinion.
8. **Resource Constraint Reality:** The organization operates without a dedicated, internal cybersecurity department, meaning all planned remediations and target dates must be scaled to fit the operational bandwidth of the existing IT and engineering teams.
